    Speed of light in a vacuum:
      \begin{equation}
        c  =  3.0 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s} \tag{in a vacuum}
    \end{equation}
    Speed of light where $n$ is the index of refraction:
    \begin{equation}
      v = \frac{c}{n} \tag{speed of light}
    \end{equation}
    Law of refraction:
\begin{equation}
  n_1 \sin \theta_1 = n_2 \sin \theta_2 \tag{Snell's Law}
\end{equation}
When light refracts along the boundary ($n_2 > n_1$, light traveling from less to more dense):
\begin{equation}
  \theta_c = \sin^{-1} (\frac{n_2}{n_1}) \tag{Critical angle}
\end{equation}
Beam of light going through polarizing sheets:
\begin{equation}
  I_1 = \frac{1}{2} I_0 \tag{one half rule}
 \end{equation}
   Going through another sheet:
   \begin{equation}
     I = I_1 \cos^2 (\Delta \theta) \tag{cosine-squared}
    \end{equation}
    When a ray of light reflected by the film back to air undergo phase shift? \\
    Yes, undergoing a phase shift of $\pi$ ($\frac{1}{2} \lambda $) if light goes from
    a low index to high index. ($n_1 < n_2$)\\ 
    No if it goes from high to low index.($n_2 > n_3$)\\
    Minima for Destructive interference:
    \begin{equation}
      2L = m \frac{\lambda}{n_2}
    \end{equation}
    Minima for Constructive interference:
    \begin{equation}
      2L = (m+\frac{1}{2}) \frac{\lambda}{n_2}
    \end{equation}
    If in Phase:
    Minima for Destructive interference:
    \begin{equation}
      2L = (m+\frac{1}{2}) \frac{\lambda}{n_2}
    \end{equation}
    Minima for Constructive interference:
    \begin{equation}
      2L = m \frac{\lambda}{n_2}
    \end{equation}
    Double slit:
    \vspace{2em}
    $\sin \theta$ is so small that:
    \begin{equation}
      \sin \theta = \tan \theta = \frac{y_m}{D}
    \end{equation}
    interference Minima:
    \begin{equation}
      d \sin \theta = (m+\tfrac{1}{2}) \lambda
      \end{equation}
interference Maxima: 
\begin{equation}
  d \sin \theta = m\lambda
  \end{equation}
  Path Difference (use $m + \tfrac{1}{2}$ if looking for ):
  \begin{equation}
    \Delta L = d \sin \theta = m \lambda
  \end{equation}
Phase Difference:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta \theta = \frac{2 \pi}{\lambda}\Delta L
\end{equation}
Spacing between adjacent bright fringes:
\begin{equation}
  y_m1 - y_m0 = \lambda \frac{D}{d}
\end{equation}

Find width of central bright fringe:\\
Remember that $\tan \theta = $ oppo / adj,
we can use where $2 y_1$ is the width:
\begin{equation}
 2 y_1 = 2 L \tan \theta
\end{equation}
Rayleigh's Criterion:
\begin{equation}
  \theta_R = 1.22 (\frac{\lambda}{d})
\end{equation}
Length between eye and object:
\begin{equation}
  L = \frac{Dd}{1.22 \lambda}
\end{equation}
Where D is size of object, and d is the diameter of eye.\\
Grating Size:
\begin{equation}
  d = \frac{W}{N}
\end{equation}

Focal length:
\begin{equation}
  f = \frac{1}{2} r
\end{equation}
Object distance P, image distance i, focal length f:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{p} + \frac{1}{i}
\end{equation}
Magnification:
\begin{equation}
  m = \frac{h'}{h} = \frac{-i}{p}
